## 1. Character design: turning manga costumes into anime settei (衣装設定), and the simplification rules

### Takeaway
The anime character designer (キャラクターデザイン) "refines" the mangaka's art into model sheets that keep the original's impression while cutting line density, fold count, pattern detail and color count. The main reason is that each drawing must be redrawn thousands of times by key and in-between animators on fixed per-drawing pay. Costumes are the main thing that gets simplified, and on clothing-heavy shows (e.g. *My Dress-Up Darling*) a dedicated 衣装デザイン (costume design) credit may be added.

### Cited Findings
- In anime character design, linework is reduced and similar adjustments are made so that the original's look is kept while the character becomes easier to animate ("原作の絵の雰囲気は残しつつ動かしやすいように、単純に線を減らす"). This is a director (*Pretty Cure* series) explaining why anime and manga art differ. — [Diamond Online: プリキュア手掛けたアニメ監督が解説、原作とアニメで「キャラの絵が違う」理由](https://diamond.jp/articles/-/312739) (snippet only)
- Adapting キャラクター原案 (original character concept) into a キャラクターデザイン involves "refining" the art: simplifying line density and number of colors. — [Wikipedia JA: キャラクターデザイン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AD%E3%83%A3%E3%83%A9%E3%82%AF%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%87%E3%82%B6%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3) (snippet only)
- Because anime production cannot spend much time on a single drawing, simplifying clothing within limits that keep the overall impression intact is described as important. — same search result set ([Wikipedia JA キャラクターデザイン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AD%E3%83%A3%E3%83%A9%E3%82%AF%E3%82%BF%E3%83%BC%E3%83%87%E3%82%B6%E3%82%A4%E3%83%B3) / [おとなりに銀河 character designer 大滝那佳 interview](https://otonari-anime.com/special/interview06.html)). The snippet did not say which of the two pages the sentence came from.
- A character designer has said that props in recent anime character design have become close to live-action (実写に近くなってきており), so the workload is very heavy. — reported in the same search result set (likely the *おとなりに銀河* character design interview, [otonari-anime.com interview06](https://otonari-anime.com/special/interview06.html)). Attribution is unconfirmed because the page could not be fetched.
- Manga designs can be rich in detail but are not necessarily optimized for animation. Designers may tweak them in ways that favor animation, such as rounder faces. Designs must be economical with detail, but "simply making drawings simple" is not enough without clear references for how the design behaves in different situations. Clothing that visibly behaves as a separate layer from the body, rather than "magically attaching itself", is praised as telling the viewer about the wearer. — [Sakuga Blog search results incl. Koe no Katachi: Character Designer Futoshi Nishiya Interview](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2017/06/02/koe-no-katachi-character-designer-chief-animation-director-futoshi-nishiya-interview/); [Sakuga Blog: The Pre-Production of Anime #3: Design Work](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2017/07/21/the-pre-production-of-anime-3-design-work/) (snippet only; which statement comes from which article is unconfirmed)
- *My Dress-Up Darling* (その着せ替え人形は恋をする, CloverWorks, 2022; S2 2025). Credits: director 篠原啓輔 (Keisuke Shinohara), character design and chief animation director 石田一将 (Kazumasa Ishida; one snippet garbled this as "Ishida Ichimasaru"), **衣装デザイン 西原恵利香 (Erika Nishihara)**. Reports say Nishihara worked with the director to simplify the manga's costume designs while keeping their impression, cutting unnecessary visual elements to control workload. — [Febri: 監督が語る…『その着せ替え人形は恋をする』の制作秘話①](https://febri.jp/topics/bisquedoll-anime1/); [Official staff page](https://bisquedoll-anime.com/staffcast/); [Wikipedia JA](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%81%9D%E3%81%AE%E7%9D%80%E3%81%9B%E6%9B%BF%E3%81%88%E4%BA%BA%E5%BD%A2%E3%81%AF%E6%81%8B%E3%82%92%E3%81%99%E3%82%8B) (snippet only)
- *My Dress-Up Darling* S2: staff say costume designer Nishihara researched how the costumes are built and helped animators understand how each cosplay outfit is constructed. She also took part in research on makeup, including differences between female and male character makeup. — [Febri: Season 2 制作スタッフたち①](https://febri.jp/topics/bisquedoll-anime-season2-1/); [Febri ②](https://febri.jp/topics/bisquedoll-anime-season2-2/); [Anime!Anime! S2 staff interview, 2026-01-29](https://animeanime.jp/article/2026/01/29/95572.html) (snippet only)
- Sakuga Blog has a translated crosstalk between Shinohara, Ishida and chief animation director Jun Yamazaki on *My Dress-Up Darling*. It is likely the best English primary source on costume simplification for this show, but it could not be fetched. — [Sakuga Blog crosstalk translation](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2023/05/05/my-dress-up-darling-crosstalk-translation-series-director-keisuke-shinohara-x-character-designer-kazumasa-ishida-x-chief-animation-director-jun-yamazaki/)
- Example from an original (non-manga) idol property with a parallel problem. *PriPara* art designer 櫻井明香 (Syn Sophia) says the designer's skill lies in cutting the number of colors and ornaments so the costume is easy to animate while keeping it cute and flashy. Designs also consider how colors read in 3DCG live scenes, e.g. color choices that suit glow-stick lighting. — [Creators Station: 『プリパラ』シリーズのアートデザイナーが語る衣装デザイン](https://www.creators-station.jp/interview/curiousity/34834); [Syn Sophia blog: 『プリパラ』のアートデザイナーのお仕事](https://blog.syn-sophia.co.jp/articles/2024/07/08/pripara10th_art); [Real Sound: 金谷有希子 interview](https://realsound.jp/book/2024/06/post-1699325_3.html) (snippet only)
- *Detective Conan* character designer and chief animation director 須藤昌朋 talks about aiming for thicker lines as the series' anime history evolved. — [anan web](https://ananweb.jp/categories/entertainment/91178) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- The simplification "rules" that came up across sources are: (a) fewer lines overall (line density 線密度), (b) fewer colors (色数), (c) fewer ornaments/accessories (装飾), (d) keep the silhouette and "impression" (印象/雰囲気) rather than literal detail. Fold/wrinkle reduction and plaid/lace simplification are implied by "fewer lines" and "simplify patterns", but no retrieved source listed them as explicit rules.
- When a show's subject is clothing (cosplay, idols, fashion), studios tend to add a dedicated costume-design credit instead of leaving it all to the character designer (*My Dress-Up Darling*: 衣装デザイン; *Revue Starlight* film: 衣裳武器デザイン, see §2).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a primary source giving explicit numeric guidance (e.g. "max N folds per sleeve" or "lace reduced to a scallop line"). Background knowledge (unverified) says such guidance usually lives in per-show 作画注意事項 (animation notes) sheets, not in public interviews.
- Full quotes from the Diamond, Febri and Sakuga Blog articles could not be checked (fetch blocked).
- No retrieved interview on costume handling for *Kaguya-sama*, *Jujutsu Kaisen* or *Frieren* character design specifically.

## 2. What a costume/outfit settei sheet contains, and costume/fashion-designer credits

### Takeaway
A settei package is the visual bible: front/side/back turnarounds, costume construction details, accessories, prop sheets, size comparisons and color settei. A distinct costume-design credit exists on some productions, and fashion professionals have been brought in on high-profile anime films (*Belle*, *The Garden of Words*), though this is rare in TV manga adaptations.

### Cited Findings
- Settei (設定, "specifications") are pre-production design documents defining the visual reference for characters, props, costumes, colors and environments. A typical package includes character sheets (turnarounds and full body front/side/back), expression sheets, size charts (relative heights and proportions), **costume details (clothing construction, accessories, hair design)**, prop sheets (weapons, vehicles, objects) and color settei (official color references and material notes). — [Animation Cel glossary: Settei](https://animation-cel.com/glossary/settei); [Sakugabooru wiki: settei](https://www.sakugabooru.com/wiki/show?title=settei) (snippet only)
- Settei is subdivided into character settei, background settei and prop settei. Historically studios made a copy that was then photocopied and handed out to animators as reference to keep things "on model" (historical practice). — [Sakuga fandom wiki: Settei](https://sakuga.fandom.com/wiki/Settei); [Wikipedia: Model sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_sheet) (snippet only)
- Settei shadow conventions: shadow areas are marked with fills, e.g. **blue fill for shadow on clothes, eyes and teeth; green/pink fill for shadow on hair; yellow for highlights**. The shadow colors relate to the material the shadow falls on and how strong it is. On rushed or secondary-character settei, shadows may just be flat blue. — [Sakuga Blog: The Pre-Production of Anime #3: Design Work](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2017/07/21/the-pre-production-of-anime-3-design-work/); [Sakuga Blog glossary](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/glossary/) (snippet only)
- *Revue Starlight* (TV 2018, Kinema Citrus): character design 齊田博之 (Hiroyuki Saita); prop design 高倉武史 and 谷紫織. For *Revue Starlight the Movie* (2021) the credit **衣裳武器デザイン (costume and weapon design) went to 小出卓史 (Takushi Koide)**. — [Revue Starlight film staff page](https://cinema.revuestarlight.com/cast-staff/); [TV site](https://revuestarlight.com/animation/tv/) (snippet only). Note that *Revue Starlight* is a media-mix original, not a manga adaptation.
- *Belle* (竜とそばかすの姫, Studio Chizu, 2021, original film): costumes came from three non-animation professionals. Stylist **伊賀大介 (Daisuke Iga)** styled and produced all costumes for both the real world and the virtual world "U". **森永邦彦 (Kunihiko Morinaga, ANREALAGE, Paris Collection)** designed Belle's two glowing concert dresses, using photochromic-bead and patchwork ideas from the brand. Flower artist **篠崎恵美 (Megumi Shinozaki)** designed the red flower-motif opening dress and others. Belle has 8 costumes in total. — [TOKION](https://tokion.jp/2021/07/15/ryu-to-sobakasu-no-hime-anrealage/); [Fashionsnap](https://www.fashionsnap.com/article/2021-07-15/ryuto-sobakasu-no-hime/); [CINRA: 伊賀大介×篠崎恵美×森永邦彦](https://www.cinra.net:443/article/interview-202107-ryutosobakasu_ysdmscl); [Cinema Today: ベルの衣装は8種類](https://www.cinematoday.jp/news/N0124952); [ANREALAGE work page](https://www.anrealage.com/work/22) (snippet only)
- In Makoto Shinkai's *The Garden of Words* (言の葉の庭), a stylist was brought in to give the heroine Yukino the realism of a real person her age. A general claim was also made that professional fashion designers often take part in 衣装設定. — search snippet attributed to [teenssexandwarmode blog: 服飾で見通すアニメデザイン](https://teenssexandwarmode.hatenablog.com/entry/2014/08/11/200313) (blog, secondary; treat the "often" claim with caution)
- Anime credit lists include costume-related roles. A personal credit-role reference is at [note: アニメスタッフクレジット 職種確認表](https://note.com/morikai33/n/n190eb97123a2) (snippet did not list specifics).

### Inferences
- In TV manga adaptations, costume settei is normally drawn by the character designer, with sub-character/guest-outfit designers and prop designers (プロップデザイン) handling accessories, weapons and bags. A separate 衣装デザイン credit appears mainly when costumes are central to the story (cosplay, stage, idol) or when there are very many outfits.
- Fashion-industry participation is concentrated in original films and prestige projects (Hosoda, Shinkai) rather than manga TV adaptations.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve an actual published costume settei page description, for example from an official 設定資料集, to list exact callouts such as "fold guide" or "back of collar" diagrams. Background knowledge (unverified): settei commonly include 衣装の裏/背面 (back/inside) views, 縫い目 (seams), シワの入り方 (how wrinkles form) notes, and 色トレス (color-trace) line indications.
- Other well-known fashion collaborations (e.g. *Lupin*, *Sk8*, *Yuri!!! on Ice* skating costumes) were not verified in this session.

## 3. Color design (色彩設計): choosing clothing colors from black-and-white manga, color charts, shadow/highlight, time-of-day variants

### Takeaway
The color designer sets every color in the show (a "normal" palette plus shadow/highlight tones and scene-specific variants such as evening and night). Episode-level 色指定 staff apply and extend it cut by cut. For monochrome manga, designers rely first on the author's color illustrations and then on conventions (solid black becomes a dark color, screentone becomes a mid-value), while adjusting so characters in the same scene do not clash or blend together.

### Cited Findings
- Workflow: 色彩設計 (color design) creates the overall color specifications. 色指定 (color specification) staff indicate which colors to use for each cut. 仕上 (finishing/painting) staff apply them frame by frame. — [Studio Ghibli ゲド戦記 制作日誌 (8) 色彩設計・色指定・仕上](https://www.ghibli.jp/ged_01/30special/000611.html); [WEBアニメスタイル 辻田邦夫 色彩設計おぼえがき 第1回](http://www.style.fm/as/05_column/tsujita/tsujita01.shtml); [animator276: 色彩設計・色指定・仕上](https://animator276.com/shiage/) (snippet only)
- The color designer creates a 色彩設定表 / color specification sheet that decides all colors in the work and sets the overall direction to match the director's intent and the show's tone (e.g. bright and vivid for children's shows, muted for mystery/war). 色指定 staff then choose colors per scene based on the designer's data, considering weather, time and character emotion. — [AMG: アニメの色彩設計（色指定）とは](https://www.amg.ac.jp/animation-column/job/anime-colour/); [デジタルアーツ東京](https://www.dat.ac.jp/course/anime/anime-column/color_design/); [Wikipedia JA: 色彩設定](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%89%B2%E5%BD%A9%E8%A8%AD%E5%AE%9A) (snippet only)
- **Time-of-day variants:** color design sets character and scene colors for ノーマル (normal/day), 夕方 (evening) and 夜 (night). Evening shifts hues warm (orange/red) with slightly lower brightness. Night shifts toward blue with strongly reduced saturation and brightness. ノーマル色 is defined as the color under daytime sunlight, with separate scene colors for night and strong-light situations. — [Masako Sato (note): ノーマル色、とは](https://note.com/sa10ma/n/n9eed72859a92); [Tooniq: アニメ色彩設計パレット](https://tooniq.co.jp/tools/anime-color-palette/); [Kyoto Animation Creators' Diary](https://www.kyotoanimation.co.jp/diary/archives/2477) (snippet only; the warm/blue shift description may come from the Tooniq tool page, a less authoritative source)
- Sakuga Blog glossary: the Color Designer (色彩設定/色彩設計) sets the show's overall palette. Each episode has its own 色指定 who supervises painters and supplies the color models that outing needs, and may create new ones for tones the color designer did not define. Color designers usually set a base "normal colors" look, then adjust tones to the time and location the characters are in. — [Sakuga Blog: Color Designer](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/glossary/color-designer/) (snippet only)
- **Monochrome-manga handling:** when source material lacks color information or has inconsistent coloring, color designers unify it. Areas drawn in **solid black (ベタ) in the manga are usually given dark colors**. Color variety is sometimes adjusted so that several characters in the same scene do not end up with identical colors. Designers typically **request all color illustrations from the original work**. Where none exist for a character or costume, they fall back on conventions such as dark colors for solid-black areas. — from the [おとなりに銀河 color designer 大塚眞純 interview](https://otonari-anime.com/special/interview05.html) and related results ([Madhouse おぎにゃん 第8回 色彩設計](https://www.madhouse.co.jp/special/oginyan/oginyann_08_a.html)) (snippet only; exact quote unverified)
- Doga Kobo color designer 石黒けい (Kei Ishiguro) gave an interview on "動画工房の色" (Doga Kobo's color) in Real Sound's studio series. — [Real Sound 2024/05](https://realsound.jp/movie/2024/05/post-1670331_2.html). Content could not be retrieved.
- Sakuga Blog has translated *A Silent Voice* (Kyoto Animation) roundtables with the color coordinator, which are relevant primary material on how color is chosen. — [Koe no Katachi: Color Coordinator x Special Effects Roundtable](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2017/05/26/koe-no-katachi-color-coordinator-x-special-effects-roundtable/); [Aesthetics Team roundtable](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2020/04/30/koe-no-katachi-a-silent-voice-staff-roundtable-aesthetics-team/) (not fetched)
- *Frieren* (Madhouse, 2023): the key visual was promoted as conveying "the colors and light atmosphere of the original" (原作の色彩と光の雰囲気). Concept art was by 吉岡誠子. — [PASH! PLUS](https://www.pashplus.jp/anime/291262/); [MdN: 吉岡誠子インタビュー](https://www.mdn.co.jp/design/features/7155) (snippet only; no color-designer quote retrieved)

### Inferences
- Screentone-to-color mapping: by analogy with the documented "ベタ (solid black) becomes a dark color" rule, a mid-grey screentone most plausibly becomes a mid-value color and white areas become light colors. Color illustrations (cover/color pages) override this where they exist. No retrieved source explicitly described the screentone rule.
- Every clothing color needs at least a normal and a shadow tone (often a highlight and sometimes a second shadow too). Each scene variant (evening, night, indoor, special lighting) multiplies the number of color models per outfit. So each extra outfit adds to the color workload in proportion to the number of lighting conditions it appears in.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gives a typical number of colors per outfit, or how many time-of-day variants per outfit a TV series usually prepares.
- No direct color-designer quote was retrieved for *Spy x Family*, *Frieren*, *Kaguya-sama* or *Jujutsu Kaisen*.

## 4. Complex patterns (plaid, checkered haori, floral kimono, logos): hand-drawn vs. texture paste vs. 3DCG

### Takeaway
Three approaches exist: (1) simplify or omit the pattern, (2) composite a 2D texture onto the painted area in 撮影 (photography/compositing), or (3) hand-draw the pattern on every frame. The famous example is ufotable's *Demon Slayer*: according to widely shared staff-sourced reports, Tanjiro's checkered haori is hand-drawn with the squares counted for consistency, not texture-mapped, and this is often described as "brute force" (力技).

### Cited Findings
- *Demon Slayer* (ufotable): Tanjiro's black-and-green 市松模様 (ichimatsu/checkered) haori moves without being simplified. This is **not texture-mapping technology**: animators simply separate the lines by hand, which commentators describe as 力技 (brute force). **Animators count the black and green squares**, and the finishing (仕上げ) staff work while checking the in-betweeners' 裏塗り (back-painting indications). — [Togetter: 炭治郎の羽織の市松模様が…実は全部手描きして柄の数を数えていた「技術云々じゃなく力技」](https://togetter.com/li/1614752) (a compilation of social-media posts; snippet only; the original staff source is not identified in the snippet)
- Same compilation: on the real garment the checker pattern would be symmetric on the front but asymmetric on the back, because the pattern has to be offset when a kimono is actually sewn. The anime reportedly prioritized reproducing the manga's look and chose a "drawing lie" (作画上の嘘). — [Togetter](https://togetter.com/li/1614752) (snippet only; low-to-medium confidence)
- ufotable produces nearly everything in-house: direction, character animation, backgrounds, 3DCG and compositing (撮影). Its 撮影 team (director 寺尾優一 Yuichi Terao) and 3D director 西脇一樹 (Kazuki Nishiwaki) describe close communication between departments. Its compositing of characters and backgrounds, including lighting and atmosphere adjustments, is a known strength. — [Autodesk AREA: 世界を虜にしたアニメ『鬼滅の刃』はどう作られたのか 前編](https://area.autodesk.jp/case/animation/kimetsu-01/); [Bunshun: 『鬼滅の刃』の絵がスゴイのは、"作画"よりも〇〇の力？](https://bunshun.jp/articles/-/42113); [MANTANWEB](https://mantan-web.jp/article/20201018dog00m200015000c.html) (snippet only)
- The 2026 making-of for *Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle* (劇場版 無限城編) was framed around ufotable's "overwhelming inefficiency" (圧倒的非効率), meaning labor-intensive methods. — [GameBusiness.jp 2026-01-14](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/01/14/25960.html) (headline-level only)
- The patterns in *Demon Slayer* (Tanjiro's 市松, Nezuko's 麻の葉 asanoha, etc.) are traditional Japanese motifs with symbolic meanings, explained by kimono specialists. — [きものと / 京都きもの市場 コラム](https://www.kimonoichiba.com/media/column/373/); [All About](https://allabout.co.jp/gm/gc/486132/) (snippet only)
- Illustration-practice sources note that a pasted texture looks flat unless it is warped to the garment's form and folds. For pleated plaid skirts, the number of checks per pleat must match. Hand-drawn plaid that follows the form gives better depth. — [ichi-up: テクスチャの貼り方講座](https://ichi-up.net/2015/033); [mangania: テクスチャではだめ！自分でチェック柄を描いてこそ出せる立体感](https://cs.mangania.jp/archives/5847); [CLIP STUDIO: 服に合わせて柄を入れたい](https://www.clipstudio.net/oekaki/archives/151601). These are illustration/manga tutorials, not anime-studio sources.
- *Love Live!* (Sunrise; 3DCG live scenes by Sanzigen in the first TV series): live costumes were reportedly **modeled per character rather than done as textures**. — [Wikipedia JA: サンジゲン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B8%E3%82%B2%E3%83%B3) and related search snippet (snippet only)

### Inferences
- Hand-drawing a pattern keeps it locked to folds and motion, but it multiplies line count and the in-betweener's and painter's work on every frame. The Togetter description of counting squares and checking 裏塗り shows the cost lands on 動画 (in-betweening) and 仕上げ (painting), not only on key animation.
- Background knowledge (unverified): many TV productions handle plaid skirts and intricate kimono with 撮影-stage texture overlays (テクスチャ貼り/パターン貼り込み) or simply drop the pattern on small or fast shots. No retrieved source in this session documented a specific TV title doing so.

### Gaps
- No primary ufotable staff quote on the haori was retrieved. The "count the squares" claim rests on a Togetter compilation.
- No retrieved source on how *Kaguya-sama* (Shuchiin uniform), *Oshi no Ko* or *Jujutsu Kaisen* handle uniform patterns or logos.

## 5. Cloth motion: sakuga fabric animation, secondary motion, follow-through, wind

### Takeaway
Cloth in hand-drawn anime is animated as secondary motion (follow-through, lag, wave and settle) driven by the body and wind. It is a showcase element of sakuga, but this session turned up little sourced material naming specific cloth-specialist animators.

### Cited Findings
- Cloth "gathers, stretches, compresses, and flows according to the speed of the body beneath it and the resistance of the air". Fabric animation (flags, cloth, dresses in wind) is among the most memorable parts of animation. — [Sakuga Blog: A Look at the Sakuga and Animation of 2018](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/2018/12/30/a-look-at-the-sakuga-and-animation-of-2018/) / [fullfrontal.moe Sakuga Espresso](https://fullfrontal.moe/sakuga-espresso/) (snippet only; general statement, source attribution within result set uncertain)
- Secondary animation means elements (hair, cloth, tails, loose accessories, fabric folds) that follow, react to and echo the primary movement. The animator chooses how much lag, how much wave, how quickly things settle, and whether the motion amplifies or contrasts with the main action. — [Animworks: Secondary Animation](https://anim.works/secondary-animation/) (Western animation education source)
- 原画 (genga / key animation) covers layout and the cleaned-up key drawings. 作画監督 corrections are applied on top. — [Sakuga fandom wiki: Genga](https://sakuga.fandom.com/wiki/Genga)
- *Revue Starlight* TV episode 1 analysis notes attention to how skirts are drawn in motion (e.g. avoiding visible underwear during action). — [映画の後には紅茶とお菓子を: 『少女☆歌劇 レヴュースタァライト』第1話の感想と、演出と作画](https://films.hatenablog.com/entry/2018/07/14/220000) (fan blog, snippet only)
- *Spy x Family* (Wit Studio/CloverWorks): Yor's all-black outfit (on merchandise reproductions of official art) is described with carefully detailed wrinkles, a billowing skirt, a rose pattern inside the skirt lining, and glossy black boots that contrast with the dress fabric. — [Dengeki Hobby](https://hobby.dengeki.com/news/1676680/) (this describes a figure based on art, not the anime's settei)

### Inferences
- In TV production, cloth secondary motion is where ambition meets budget. Fluttering coats, capes and haori in action scenes need more in-betweens and more careful drawing, so directors put them in climactic cuts and often minimize them elsewhere (background knowledge, not sourced here).

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve a sourced list of key animators known specifically for cloth. Candidates from background knowledge (unverified in this session) include Yutaka Nakamura (effects and action), Mitsuo Iso, Shinya Ohira, and ufotable's in-house action animators. The report writer should not cite these without verification.
- No retrieved interview where an animator explains their method for fabric timing.

## 6. Consistency management: 作画監督 corrections, outfit continuity, many outfits per character, settei libraries

### Takeaway
Consistency is enforced by checking layouts and key drawings against the settei. The episode director checks first, then the 作画監督 (animation director) redraws off-model elements on correction paper. On many productions a 総作画監督 (chief animation director) does a further pass. Settei, including every outfit variant, is the reference used at each stage.

### Cited Findings
- The 作画監督's core job is checking and correcting layouts and key animation. Layouts pass the episode director (演出) first, then the 作画監督, who compares them with the storyboard and **settei materials** to catch drawing errors and character-design deviations. Corrections (赤入れ) are drawn on correction paper laid over the key drawing, not by erasing it, and full redraws (全修正) happen. Directors, sakkan and key animators hold briefings (打ち合わせ) with storyboards and settei (character and background) in hand. — [KADOKAWA アニメ・声優アカデミー: 作画監督になるには](https://school.kadokawa.co.jp/anime/column/how-to-become-an-animation-director/); [yoani: 作画監督になるには](https://www.yoani.co.jp/gyokainavi-top/animator/an-works/ad-how-to/); [animator276: 作画作業の工程](https://animator276.com/animation_work/sakuga-kotei/) (snippet only; vocational school and practitioner sources)
- *Spy x Family* credits character design to 嶋田和晃 (Kazuaki Shimada), with 浅野恭司 (Kyoji Asano) and Shimada as 総作画監督 (chief animation directors). — [Wikipedia JA: SPY×FAMILY (アニメ)](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPY%C3%97FAMILY_(%E3%82%A2%E3%83%8B%E3%83%A1)); [Wit Studio works page](https://www.witstudio.co.jp/works/2022/04/636.html) (snippet only; the snippet garbled the name as "Shimada Kazuakinari")
- Fan-documented 作画ミス (drawing mistakes) such as wrong outfit details are a recognized category. — [pixiv百科事典: 作画ミス](https://dic.pixiv.net/a/%E4%BD%9C%E7%94%BB%E3%83%9F%E3%82%B9) (snippet only)
- 色指定 staff per episode supply the painters with the color models each episode needs and create new ones when needed. This keeps outfit colors consistent across episodes. — [Sakuga Blog: Color Designer glossary](https://blog.sakugabooru.com/glossary/color-designer/)

### Inferences
- Outfit continuity relies on (1) the settei library growing as new outfits appear (guest and sub-character outfits are designed per episode), (2) the 作画監督 and 総作画監督 correction passes, and (3) the 色指定's color-model management. Each new outfit adds a line and a color settei. On a cosplay show like *My Dress-Up Darling*, this is why a dedicated costume designer was credited.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source quantified how many outfit settei a typical cour contains, or how outfit continuity errors are tracked (e.g. continuity sheets).

## 7. 3DCG cloth and hybrid 2D/3D clothing in modern TV anime

### Takeaway
3DCG is used for clothing mainly in idol and live scenes and in full-CG cel-look productions. *Love Live!* set the template of hand-drawn close-ups with 3DCG wide shots where full-body skirt motion is needed. Cel-look studios such as Sanzigen use limited-frame 3DCG with toon shading, and add cloth simulation for big action.

### Cited Findings
- *Love Live!* (Sunrise, 2013–14) is described as the first case of a hybrid show mixing hand-drawn and 3DCG for the same characters. Basic stance: **"bust-ups are hand-drawn; wide shots that include from the skirt down are 3DCG"**. Within one frame, foreground characters may be hand-drawn and background characters 3DCG. — [法華狼の日記: 『ラブライブ！』（第2期）#9ライブシーンに見る、手描き作画から3DCGへの移行](https://hokke-ookami.hatenablog.com/entry/20140613/1402671674); [足元の足元を広げる blog](https://smith-kun.blog.ss-blog.jp/2013-08-16-1) (fan analysis blogs, snippet only)
- *Love Live!* live costumes: Sanzigen handled the 3DCG live scenes, and each character's live costume was **built as a model rather than as textures**. CG studio **Sublimation** also worked on the *Love Live!* series (Sunrise produced *Sunshine!!*). — [Wikipedia JA: サンジゲン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B8%E3%82%B2%E3%83%B3); [CGWORLD studio DB: サブリメイション](https://cgworld.jp/stdatabase/21115.html) (snippet only)
- Sanzigen specializes in "cel-look 3DCG" and combines limited animation at 8 drawings per second with cel-look shading so 3DCG reads like hand-drawn animation. — [Wikipedia JA: サンジゲン](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%B5%E3%83%B3%E3%82%B8%E3%82%B2%E3%83%B3) (snippet only)
- *ULTRAMAN* (Production I.G × Sola Digital Arts, full 3DCG): for clothing, ordinary scenes use the rigged setup with small tweaks, while **big action scenes are handled with cloth simulation**. — [Anime!Anime!: セルルックとリアルルックを両立させるコツは？ 3DCGアニメ「ULTRAMAN」制作秘話](https://animeanime.jp/article/2019/11/09/49548.html) (snippet only; not a manga-to-TV cel production but illustrates the cloth-sim decision rule)
- CGWORLD covered hybrid hand-drawn + 3DCG live scenes for *Uta no Prince-sama Maji LOVE Legend Star* (3 groups, 18 characters) and *THE IDOLM@STER MILLION LIVE!* (52 idols drawn with "実在感", a sense of real presence). — [CGWORLD: うたプリ](https://cgworld.jp/feature/201706-cgw227-utapri-2.html); [CGWORLD: アイドルマスター ミリオンライブ！](https://cgworld.jp/article/202312-cgw303-imml.html) (headline-level only; contents not retrieved)
- *Girls Band Cry* (Toei, 2024) is discussed as a 3DCG look somewhat different from typical cel-look CG. — [悠々自適 blog](https://yuu-1101.hatenablog.com/entry/2024/07/02/190744) (fan blog)
- *PriPara* costume designers pick colors with the 3DCG live renders in mind, e.g. how glow-stick colors read. — [Creators Station](https://www.creators-station.jp/interview/curiousity/34834) (snippet only)

### Inferences
- The reason 3DCG takes over in idol wide shots is costume complexity times choreography times number of characters. Hand-drawing frilly skirts on 9+ dancing characters is prohibitively expensive, while a modeled costume can be reused across every live scene in a franchise.
- Studios named in the brief (Orange, Polygon Pictures) work on full-CG productions such as *Beastars* and *Land of the Lustrous* (Orange) and *Knights of Sidonia* (Polygon). They were not covered by retrieved sources in this session.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source on Orange, Polygon Pictures or ufotable 3DCG cloth specifically.
- No retrieved source confirms how *Oshi no Ko*'s idol live scenes split 2D and 3D. One snippet claimed the S1 B-Komachi live used largely hand-drawn animation ([ABEMA Times](https://times.abema.tv/articles/-/10132960?page=1)), but it garbled staff names (the anime's director is 平牧大輔 Daisuke Hiramaki; the snippet's "Taido Hiraaki" and "Hiranya Hirayama" are errors), so it is unreliable. Febri ran a Hiramaki interview series ([Febri: 平牧大輔が語る②](https://febri.jp/topics/oshinoko_imamaki_02/)) and MANTANWEB has another ([MANTANWEB 2024-08-16](https://mantan-web.jp/article/20240816dog00m200078000c.html)). ## 9. Cost and time impact of complex clothing

### Takeaway
Animators are mostly paid per drawing or cut regardless of how many lines each drawing has. Complex clothing therefore raises the real labor (and fatigue and schedule risk) per drawing without raising pay. This is a structural reason character designers simplify costumes. Retrieved sources gave rate ranges but no costume-specific cost figures.

### Cited Findings
- 動画 (in-between) unit rates are roughly **¥280–400 per drawing**, or **¥150–280** when outsourced. — [レバテッククリエイター: アニメーターの単価](https://creator.levtech.jp/tips/article/195/) (snippet only; secondary source, date not confirmed)
- Drawing difficulty and quality are generally **not reflected in pay**: a cut that only moves a mouth and a dynamic action cut pay the same if they use the same number of drawings. — same search result set ([levtech](https://creator.levtech.jp/tips/article/195/) / [Togetter 原画1枚4000円 correction](https://togetter.com/li/1180906)) (snippet only)
- "Quality" in anime is described mainly as **画面情報量 (screen information density: number of lines and complexity of the image) × 作画枚数 (number of drawings)**. — same result set ([note: アニメーション制作費がいまだに安いという日本的な事情](https://note.com/taneri777/n/nddb3217e1453)) (snippet only)
- An industry-blog snippet says per-drawing price differences mean simple backgrounds or fewer characters per frame cost less, while complex scenes with many characters cost more. — same result set (attribution uncertain)
- ufotable's approach to *Demon Slayer* is framed (2026 *Infinity Castle* making-of) as "overwhelming inefficiency", i.e. deliberately spending labor where others economize. — [GameBusiness.jp](https://www.gamebusiness.jp/article/2026/01/14/25960.html)
- A producer-written note discusses TV anime per-episode budget negotiations and a "¥15M wall" per episode. — [note: アニメ1話の制作費はいくら？](https://note.com/sou1849/n/n01638fa1cba7) (snippet only; secondary)

### Inferences
- With pay fixed per drawing, a costume with twice the lines roughly halves an animator's effective hourly rate. That leads to (a) designers simplifying up front, (b) animators unofficially simplifying in rushed cuts, and (c) the 作画監督 having to redraw missing detail, a hidden cost moved onto supervisors. This follows from the cited pay structure. No source in this session measured it.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source gives a measured time or cost difference for a complex vs. simple costume (e.g. minutes per drawing, or extra budget for a patterned outfit).
- Rate figures come from secondary career-advice sites, and recent (2024–2026) rate reforms were not verified.
